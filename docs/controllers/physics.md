## 1. Mission
Deliver believable, stable, time-warpable orbital flight and rigid-body vehicle/structure physics — KSP-grade credibility — within the active/on-rails framework, while staying cheap enough to coexist with the factory sim.

## 2. Scope & owned subsystems
- **Orbital mechanics** — patched conics (two-body + SOI switching), analytic propagation for on-rails bodies/vessels.
- **Rigid-body vehicle physics** — multi-part craft, joints, thrust, mass/CoM, aerodynamics & drag in atmospheres.
- **Surface & character physics** — walking, vehicles on terrain, collision against world-gen terrain.
- **SOI transitions** — handing a vessel between reference frames as it crosses sphere-of-influence boundaries (with [core-engine](core-engine.md)).
- **Active↔on-rails conversion** — produce/consume orbital element sets so a vessel can "park" on rails and resume under physics.
- **Non-goals:** the coordinate/frame substrate (→ core-engine); rendering of trajectories (→ rendering/gameplay map view).

| PH-8 | **`CAPSULE.stepUpM` = 1.1 m is the heightfield's step-up AND its wall**: a horizontal move onto ground more than that above where the feet started the tick is refused, sliding along one tangent axis first. | PH-10 | **The atmosphere is ONE exponential, `rho = rho0 * exp(-h/H)`, with Forge at rho0 = 1.225 kg/m^3 and H = 5600 m, ceiling 60 km.** Isothermal, so `p/p0 == rho/rho0` and one curve serves both drag and engine lapse. This is also DW-30's "kept real" list item: density genuinely falls off, so drag and Isp genuinely change as you climb. | **Accepted** | 2026-07-26 |
| PH-11 | **Density FADES to exactly zero over the top 10 km (50 to 60 km) rather than being truncated at the ceiling.** | The raw exponential at 60 km is **2.723e-5 kg/m^3**, which is **0.072 m/s^2** on a 2 t vessel at orbital speed. A hard cut therefore puts a step in the force at exactly the altitude every player circularises at: an orbit 100 m below the line decays and one 100 m above it is eternal. The fade buys three things and costs one: density is continuous everywhere (worst measured step **3.7e-8 kg/m^3 per metre**), it is EXACTLY 0.0 above the ceiling so "am I on rails" is a boolean rather than a threshold, and the raw exponential is bit-identical below 50 km where the whole ascent drag integral lives. The cost is slightly less drag in a band the vehicle crosses in about 12 seconds. | **Accepted** | 2026-07-26 |
| PH-12 | **DW-30 item 1 implemented as: DESTABILISING pitching moment is multiplied by `AeroTuning::unstableAeroGain`, which ships at 0.0. RESTORING moment is untouched.** The branch is the sign of the static margin `(CoP - CoM) . forward`. | Three properties, all measured. **(1) It is continuous**: the moment is proportional to the static margin, so it is exactly zero where the gain switches (swept the fins through the CoM in 400 steps, worst torque jump under 6 kN·m, torque at the crossing under 6 kN·m from both sides). **(2) Fins still do real work**, because a finned vehicle is on the restoring branch and gets the full physical moment. **(3) At 0.0 the claim is a theorem, not a race between two coefficients**: rate damping alone CANNOT hold the ANGLE of a statically unstable body, it bounds the rate and lets the angle grow linearly, so the only honest ways to a bounded angle are to remove the divergence or to fly a controller, and DW-30 asks for the former. | PH-13 | **Aerodynamic rate damping is a real derivative, generously sized**: `M = -k * 0.5 * rho * v * CdA_normal * L^2 * omega_perp`, k = 0.25 pitch / 0.05 roll. | Real vehicles have pitch damping (a rotating body meets its own rotation as a distributed angle of attack along its length) and KSP models it thinly. It is what settles a gust rather than letting the vehicle ring. | **Accepted** | 2026-07-26 |
| PH-14 | **The centre of pressure is weighted by NORMAL-FORCE SLOPE (Barrowman), not by broadside drag area.** A nose gets `2 x` its frontal area, a constant-diameter body tube gets `0.15 x` its side profile, an aerodynamic surface gets `2.5 x` its planform. | Broadside area was tried first and is wrong in a way that matters: a 12 m tube of 16 m^2 of flank drowns out 3.7 m^2 of fin, so **four fins moved the CoP 0.87 m and left the reference rocket statically unstable**. With the correct coefficient the same four fins move it **4.117 m** and the margin flips from **+3.188 m to -0.884 m**. Broadside area answers "how hard does the air push a vehicle flying sideways"; normal-force slope answers "where does the pushing start when it is barely off axis". They are different distributions and a rocket needs both. | **Accepted** | 2026-07-26 |
| PH-15 | **Axial drag is NOT a sum over parts.** `CdA_axial = Cd(nose) * A_max + 0.004 * wetted area + unshaded radial parts`. The BROADSIDE sum stays a sum. | Parts in a stack SHADE one another: air meets the nose and then flows along the tube. Summed, the 12 m reference rocket had `Cd*A = 3.274 m^2` on a 1.227 m^2 disc, an **effective Cd of 2.67, roughly a parachute**, spending **64 kN of drag at max-q against 180 kN of thrust**. It still reached orbit, which is exactly why it needed a number rather than a look. Now **0.887 m^2, effective Cd 0.72**. Broadside is left as a sum and that is not inconsistent: flying sideways, every part is in the flow. Consequence worth keeping: a nose cone (Cd 0.12) against a bare capsule (0.55) cuts total drag by about 60%, so the part earns its place. | **Accepted** | 2026-07-26 |
| PH-16 | **The `flight.h` / `orbital.h` boundary is a BIT-EXACT reduction, not a tolerance.** Above the atmosphere ceiling with the engine shut, thrust and drag are exactly zero and `FlightSim::step` is written in the same term order as `orbital::Integrator::step`, so the two produce identical bits. `onRailsEligible()` is the published predicate. | A tolerance would let the two paths drift apart silently and would make "which one is right" unanswerable. ## 4. Architecture & approach
- **Two regimes:** (a) **On-rails** — vessel = Keplerian elements in its dominant frame; advance analytically, including under time-warp; (b) **Active** — full rigid-body sim near the player. core-engine's `SimProxy` triggers promotion/demotion; physics fills the content of each side and must make the handoff smooth (KSP's load-in wobble is the cautionary tale).
- **Patched conics:** vessel feels gravity from exactly one body (its SOI). Crossing an SOI boundary = a frame switch event (core-engine) + re-derivation of elements relative to the new body. Predicted trajectories (for the map/maneuver UI) computed by propagating conics forward and intersecting SOIs.
- **Vehicle structure:** parts as rigid bodies joined by constraints; thrust applied at engine parts; aero forces from atmosphere density (world-gen) × velocity. Joint stability/wobble is a known KSP pain — consider rigidified part-trees or substepping.
- **Surface:** character controller + vehicle contact against world-gen collision (heightfield, or voxel patch where dug). Gravity from the local body.

- **R12 (CLOSED as far as physics can close it, PH-36):** the flight entrance is a **roll-out stand-in**, not DW-29's launch pad, and it now says so on screen in those words. DW-29's real entrance is a pad gated behind ground progression, which does not exist at any layer (gameplay R9); **the gate remains an Admin sequencing call and physics should not invent one.**
- **R13 (NEW, for persistence/Admin, and it is bigger than R11):** **the player's own POSITION is not in the save either.** `SaveSlot` has no player key and `Boot.ts:166` teleports to `cfg.scenario` on every boot, so a reload returns you to the spawn point wherever you were and whatever you were doing, flight or no flight. Measured: `reload.mjs` went in at lat 1.613 lon 107.822 and came back at lat 2.000 lon 144.000. The base, the tunnels, the pack and the hotbar all come back correctly; only the person does not. That is a two-field addition to `SaveSlot` and it is not physics' file.
- **R14 (NEW, cosmetic, for whoever owns the readout next):** on the pad and for the first seconds of a vertical climb the trajectory is very nearly RADIAL, so `e` is 1 to within rounding and `/core` classifies it as unbound: **AP and PE read `---` for about the first ten seconds of every launch**, which KSP fills in immediately. It is correct arithmetic on a degenerate conic and it is not what a player expects. A radial ellipse has a perfectly good apoapsis and computing it is a special case in `summarize()`, in `/core`, not in the client.

### 8.1 The demo script, and what a person can safely do to it

Written for whoever is standing behind the keyboard. Every line below was
driven, not reasoned about, by `probes/flightabuse.js` (14/14 rows) and
`tools/smoke/reload.mjs` (pad, orbit and ground).

**The path:** C builds a rocket in the bay. G rolls it out 26 m ahead and says
it is a stand-in for a launch pad. Walk to it, G again to board. Shift or Z
opens the throttle, Space lights the first stage and releases the clamp, W and S
fly the nose onto the guidance marker, Space again when the first stage runs
dry, X cuts at an 80 km apoapsis, Y cycles SAS, P and O warp, G gets out once
it is down.

**Verified safe to do wrong:** mash Space on the pad (refused, and it says
throttle up first); throttle up with no engine lit (clamp holds, TWR shown);
Escape, Tab, V and left-click in flight (nothing, and the click cannot dig a
crater under the parked avatar below); open the assembly bay at 20 km and close
it again (the flight continues, and G is deliberately inert while the bay owns
the screen, so C then G is the way out); try to climb out at altitude (refused);
fly retrograde under power (no tumble, no NaN); mash the warp ladder to its top
and back; abort the ascent and ride the whole re-entry at 1000x without touching
a key (48 telemetry samples inside the atmosphere, a real 139 kPa peak, deepest
AGL 0.0 m); stage and throttle a spent vehicle. **After all of it the player
gets out, walks and digs.**

**Known rough edges a viewer may notice, none of them faults:** AP and PE read
`---` for about the first ten seconds of a launch (R14, the trajectory is
radial and `/core` calls a radial conic unbound); the HUD says regime ASCENT at
80 km (R10, `ORBIT_START` is 100 km); and the standing amber chip says the
flight is not saved, which is true and is the point (R11).
