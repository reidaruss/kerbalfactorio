## 1. Mission
Procedurally generate believable, deterministic-from-seed planets and moons: terrain, biomes, resource deposits, and points of interest — streamable as LOD chunks, deformable where needed, and cheap to persist (seed + diffs).

## 2. Scope & owned subsystems
- **Cubed-sphere quadtree terrain** (KSP PQS-style) — six quadtree-subdivided cube faces on a sphere; noise-driven heightfield; streaming LOD.
- **Biomes & surface materials** — climate/latitude/altitude driven; feeds rendering materials + gameplay science.
- **Resource deposits** — seeded node placement; per-body resource identity (this moon = X-rich) to drive logistics/exploration.
- **Voxel/SDF deformation patches** — overlay only where players dig; keep heightfield for the untouched bulk.
- **POI / structure placement** — ruins, anomalies, loot sites (content authored by [gameplay](gameplay.md); placement here).
- **Body parameters** — radius, gravity (μ), atmosphere profile, rotation — shared with physics/rendering.
- **Non-goals:** rendering the terrain (→ rendering consumes our data); what loot/quests POIs contain (→ gameplay).

## 4. Architecture & approach
- **Terrain:** noise stack (continents → mountains → detail) evaluated per cubed-sphere quad at the LOD needed by camera distance; quads stream in/out. Everything derives from `(bodySeed, faceId, quadPath)` → fully regenerable, so the natural world costs ~zero to save.
- **Deformation:** when a player digs, instantiate a voxel/SDF patch seeded from the heightfield at that location; store the *delta* only. Rendering & physics query "is there a patch here?" before falling back to heightfield. (v1 may stub this; keep the seam in the API.)
- **Deposits/POIs:** a deterministic placement pass over each body (Poisson-disk / hashed-grid by seed) yields node positions + types; gameplay binds content. Depletion/looted flags are diffs.
- **Floating-origin aware:** all queries accept/return data in core-engine's coordinate/frame terms; chunk origins are 64-bit, local meshes are near-origin.

A binary lattice can only approximate a smooth surface to within half a cell diagonal, so the shell stands up to **0.87 m proud** of the walkable ground (measured: air 0.15 m above the surface reads solid on 60.6% of walking ticks — ARCHITECTURE 15.2 item 93). **Position: neither offered option is right.** Changing the quantisation in `surface_field.h` CANNOT succeed, because the mismatch is not a bad threshold, it is inherent to the representation: any rule leaves a boundary layer up to one cell thick that one shape claims and the other does not, so moving the threshold moves WHERE the disagreement is and not WHETHER it exists (and it would re-baseline every voxel pin for nothing). Per-consumer reconciliation is what produced five surfaces: `VoxelCollision.solidForWalker` is correct for the walker and is the SIXTH definition unless it is promoted. **The third option: name both shapes in the oracle and make the choice a parameter rather than a rediscovery.** `surface_field.h` should publish `solidCell` (the VOXEL shell — what the mesher draws and what a dig carves; you must draw whole cells) alongside a `solidBelowSurface` (the shell INTERSECTED with the smooth surface — what a body occupies), with a header note stating the bound explicitly, and a ctest that ASSERTS it: for a sample of directions the two answers may differ by at most half a cell diagonal.
